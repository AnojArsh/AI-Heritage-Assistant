**Assistant:** Byzantine architecture is a style that developed in the Eastern Roman Empire, centered on Constantinople, from the 4th century CE onward. It's characterized by large central domes supported by pendentives, extensive use of mosaics with gold backgrounds, and round arches.

One of its most famous examples is the Hagia Sophia in Istanbul, which is widely considered a masterpiece of Byzantine architecture. The building was originally constructed as a church in 537 CE and later converted into a mosque after the Ottoman conquest. ## Session: demo_session_4

**User:** What's the difference between Art Deco and Bauhaus styles?

**Assistant:** Art Deco and Bauhaus styles are quite distinct. While both emerged in the 1920s-1930s, they have different underlying philosophies.

Bauhaus is all about functional simplicity, clean geometric forms, and a focus on industrial materials like steel, glass, and concrete. It's characterized by flat roofs, unornamented facades, open floor plans, and an emphasis on function over decoration.

Art Deco, on the other hand, is marked by bold geometric patterns, symmetry, stepped or "ziggurat" forms, and a lavish use of materials like chrome, glass, and stainless steel. It reflects the machine-age optimism of the interwar period with stylized motifs like sunbursts, zigzags, and streamlined forms.

In short, Bauhaus is about minimalism and functionality, while Art Deco is about glamour and luxury!
